# Limitations, Verification Boundaries & Real-vs-Metaphor (`LIMITATIONS.md`)

This document states explicitly what our Lean 4 formalization and numerical verification **do** establish, what they **do not** establish, and where literal mathematical claims stop.

## 2. Specific Technical & Process Limitations

1. **No external peer review yet:** The manuscript is an independent research preprint. It has NOT yet undergone journal peer review, arXiv moderation, or Palomar Registry acceptance.
2. **Human line-by-line fidelity audit & external operator replication are open:** Although `Challenge.lean` is only 149 lines of Mathlib-only Lean 4 (with a Lean 4.35 `module` header, 9 targets) and `NonVacuity.lean` proves 20 concrete small-$L$, small-$n$, and adversarial checks, no independent third-party mathematician has yet signed off line-by-line on `docs/statement-fidelity.md` or logged an external clean-room replication (`B44: partial` in `repo.yaml`).
3. **Lean 4.35.0 `module` system & Palomar readiness:** As of `v2.4.0`, all 10 Lean source files carry Lean 4.35.0 `module` headers (`public import` + `@[expose] public section`) and compile with **zero compiler or Mathlib style-linter warnings** (`weak.linter.mathlibStandardSet = true`) under both `lake build` and 6-kernel `lake comparator --paranoid`, satisfying Palomar Registry's file-level `module` and size rules (`Challenge.lean` 149 lines / ~6.9 KiB, longest file 3,574 lines).
4. **Windows vs. Linux sandbox for `lake comparator`:** On Linux (`ubuntu-24.04` CI), `lake comparator --config comparator.json --paranoid` executes all 6 kernels (`Lean paranoid`, `lean4lean`, `nanoda`, `con-leche`, `con-ron`, `Lean default`) inside a `bubblewrap` kernel namespace sandbox. Upstream Lean 4.35.0 `lake comparator` requires Linux `bubblewrap` and does not run directly on native Windows; on Windows, 5 of the 6 bundled kernels (`lean 4.35.0`, `leanchecker-paranoid`, `nanoda_bin`, `con-leche`, `con-ron`) can be invoked directly on `leanexport` output while full 6-kernel verification (including `lean4lean`) runs in Linux containers or Ubuntu 24.04 CI.
5. **AI-assisted provenance:** Mathematical exploration, Lean 4 proof engineering, Python test scaffolding, and LaTeX drafting were conducted with AI assistance under Alexander S. Donahue's direction (see `formalization.yaml` and the paragraph *"AI assistance, CRediT authorship, conflicts of interest, and errata policy"* in `paper/main.tex`). Epistemic warrant rests strictly on the reproducible 6-kernel verification and independent exact calculations, never on author or model authority.
6. **Export-control self-screen (EAR 15 CFR 734.8 / ITAR 22 CFR 120):** This repository contains solely published fundamental mathematical research in probability and statistics (asymptotic variance of discrete total variation under the uniform multinomial law) with zero cryptographic, defense, or dual-use technical data or software.

## 3. Real vs. Metaphor

- Every mathematical term in this repository (`multinomial distribution`, `discrete total variation`, `Euler–Maclaurin summation`, `Stirling expansion`, `asymptotic variance`, `residue class`) is used strictly in its **literal, standard mathematical definition**—there are no physical, biological, or heuristic metaphors.
- The colloquial phrase **"5-second proof verification"** in `README.md` means inspecting the committed verbatim `#print axioms` block and re-running `lake env lean VerifyAxioms.lean` against pre-built `.olean` caches; a cold build without Mathlib's `lake exe cache get` takes substantially longer (~2–5 minutes with cache, >30 minutes compiling Mathlib from source).
- **"Six-kernel verified"** refers literally to executing the six independent type-checkers bundled with Lean 4.35.0 (`Lean 4.35.0` C++ kernel, `leanchecker-paranoid`, `lean4lean`, `nanoda 0.4.19`, `con-leche`, `con-ron`) over `Challenge.lean` and `Solution.lean`; it does **not** eliminate the need to inspect `Challenge.lean` itself for statement fidelity against Banić & Elezović (2026).
